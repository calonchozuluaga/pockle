Pockle UI assessment

Pockle has two looks. Where a painting fills the screen and the toys stand in it, it looks like a finished product: the sign-up screens, the collection covers, Play. Everywhere else it is a peach page of flat rounded boxes with sentences in them. The work is to make the second look match the first.

You named three problems: too many text buttons, messy screens, not enough custom art. The screens bear all three out, and they share a cause. The art you have is spent on the things you collect (toys, boxes, covers, backdrops). None of it is spent on the app around them: the buttons, cards, bars, icons and backgrounds are all drawn by code as plain shapes.

Why it feels cheap

Six causes, in order of how much they cost the app.

1

A flat frame around glossy toys

Every button, card, tile and bar is the same flat rounded rectangle in one of three tints. The toys are lit and glossy; nothing they sit on is. This was deliberate. The look you approved in October was "flat, no shadows, the toys provide the depth". On real screens it reads as unfinished. It is the main thing I would reverse.

2

Actions are written out as sentences

The code builds buttons in 94 places. Six are icons. Leave out the toy and box tiles, and nearly everything else is words in a pill, many of them whole sentences: "Play with Peach Jelly", "View contents & probabilities", "Make your profile yours".

3

A third of every screen never moves

The title and its subtitle stay fixed at the top of each tab, and the tab bar stays at the bottom. What is left for content is 64% of the screen. On Home that is one card and the top of a second.

4

The same control, drawn six ways

Choosing between two or three options looks different on almost every screen that offers it. Toy tiles vary the same way: floating on the page in Shelf, dull pink in a collection, pastel in Pip's, faded in the picker.

5

Two of the five tabs lead almost nowhere

Walk is one card and a paragraph. Community is three "Coming soon" cards. Together they are 40% of the tab bar.

6

The big moments are the plainest screens

Opening a box and fusing two toys are the payoffs. Both happen on a near-black screen with a line of text. The sign-up screens, which a player sees once, are far better dressed.

+

Ten small flaws that add up

  • Home's featured toy is see-through.
  • Peach Jelly's play screen repeats a chip: "Sunny · Curious · Sunny".
  • Captions under locked badges are cut off.
  • Collection pages leave a blank band above the cover.
  • Missing-toy shapes overlap their rarity labels.
  • One heart icon stands for Privacy, Badges, and Badges again on Walk.
  • Titles on the birthday and name screens sit on busy leaves.
  • The fusion swirl has a jagged edge.
  • Live-shelf captions are cut off at both edges.
  • Shelf's filter tabs and Community's banner have square corners.

What already works

These stay, and they set the standard for the rest.

Home

Mixed

What is wrong

  • Seven sections are stacked with the same weight: featured toy, box, four picture tiles, live shelf, friends' activity, keep collecting, new this season.
  • The box is the reason to open the app today, and it comes second. Its card is mostly words: a label, a name, two slots, four dots, two lines about steps, a bar, then a button.
  • The featured toy is see-through. The painting shows through its body, so it looks washed out.
  • Friends' activity shows made-up sample posts in test builds and nothing in a real one. It takes a full screen of scrolling either way.

What I would do

  • One hero at the top: the box standing in a painted scene, a ring for steps, one button.
  • Cut to four sections. Friends' activity comes back when there are friends.
  • The greeting scrolls away with the page instead of staying on screen.

Shelf

Weak

What is wrong

The plan

Six steps. The first two change every screen at once; the next four rebuild the screens in the order a player meets them. Each step ends with the same 88 captures taken again, side by side with these, and a phone build for you.

0

Choose the look

Small

Before any building, I mock up two or three looks on the same three screens (Home, Shelf, Play) and you pick one, the way you approved the earlier design boards. My starting point is buttons and cards that look like the toys (soft, lit, slightly glossy), with real places behind them (a shelf, a path, a shop counter).

New art: None yet. A handful of test icons and one test button, to settle the style.

In parallel, make room on Android (see "What limits this").

1

The kit

Medium

One set of drawn pieces replaces the flat rectangle everywhere: buttons, cards, chips, one switch, the tab bar, progress bars, dialogs. A drawn icon set replaces the line icons. The fixed header scrolls away. Every screen improves at once, before any screen is redesigned.

New art: About 14 small stretchable pieces and about 40 icons.

Fixes the ten small flaws.

2

The daily loop: Home, Walk, opening a box

Large

Home gets one hero and four sections. Walk becomes a place with a path and a ring. Opening a box gets a stage, with a flourish for each rarity.

New art: Two scene paintings (Home, Walk), rays, sparkles and confetti, three rarity flourishes.

The first phone build I would ask you to judge.

3

Collecting: Shelf, one collection, Shop

Large

Shelf becomes a shelf. One tile style for toys everywhere. Shop becomes a grid with Mystery Mix first.

New art: Shelf wood and planks, a shop counter painting, price tags.

4

Playing: Play, Toy details, Fuse

Medium

Icon-and-word controls on Play and a slimmer title. Toy details trimmed. The fusion moment restaged.

New art: A sharp fusion swirl, a few sparkle pieces. Icons come from the kit.

5

The rest: You, Badges, Community, Settings, first-run fixes

Small

Row icons, badge captions, the two hard-to-read sign-up titles, and whatever you decide for Community.

New art: Four small pictures for empty states (no spares yet, offline, nothing found, coming soon).

What limits this

Android has about 1 MB of room. Build 11 only got through Google's 500 MB limit after I shrank one picture, removed an unused one and re-packed the sounds. New paintings will not fit until that is fixed. The likely fix: 77 pictures (boxes, covers, avatars, the shelf) are stored uncompressed. Compressing them should free far more room than this whole pass needs, but that is an estimate until I measure it. Some of those files are Codex's, so part of this is a request to Codex.

The toys themselves are not in this plan. How the 3D toys and their materials look is Codex's work. One request does cross over: Home needs a solid picture of the featured toy.

Pictures keep their words. A six-year-old could not read the menus in October, and you asked for labelled controls in November. So main actions become an icon with one or two words, never an icon alone.

About 75 new pieces. Most are small. The paintings are the only ones that weigh much.
