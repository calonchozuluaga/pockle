`PockleTactile.unity` contains a single `PrototypeBootstrap`. At play, it creates the controller; that controller creates the scene presentation, Pip, HUD, reveal box, and synthesized audio. Pip now loads a Blender-authored art pass, with the original procedural renderer available for comparison.

The Built-in rendering pipeline is intentional. The four custom shaders use one simple pass each and no screen grabs, AI requests, post-processing stack, or real-time shadow maps. An authored Pip also owns a small 64-pixel-per-face HDR studio cubemap generated once from broad light panels (RGBA32 fallback). Its glossy shell and eyes sample that static environment; the shell raises opacity at reflections. This is an environment reflection, not a live scene capture or physically correct refraction. Resources materials retain those shaders for builds because `Shader.Find` alone does not reliably preserve a shader. A future art pipeline can replace the procedural renderer while keeping the interaction core.

The selected editor is Unity 6.6, pinned to the owner's installed version `6000.6.5f1`. UGUI is pinned to 2.6.0, matching Unity's official 6000.6 source. Its `UnityEngine.UI` assembly name is retained, and the UI Elements module is declared for that assembly's reference. The bootstrap uses Unity 6's `FindFirstObjectByType` API. These source/package updates still require package resolution and compilation in the actual editor.

`Pockle.Core` has no engine dependency. `Spring1D` integrates an analytic damped oscillator instead of frame-sensitive Euler steps. Springs control compression, stretch, lateral lean, suspension sag, signed two-finger deformation, and three motion drives; release overshoot maps gently into the opposing deformation. `WeightedLift` uses a damped hand constraint while held and analytic gravity after release, exposing one impact-speed pulse at contact. `SuspendedShape` applies grip-supported droop to the body, face, and filling, bounded by actual plate clearance. The portable geometry function anchors the bottom, scales horizontal dimensions against the vertical change, and adds a localized pressure dent. `DirectionalStretch` adds deformation along the fingers' local separation axis with inverse radial scaling and a blended foot anchor. This approximates bulk conservation rather than simulating a physical jelly volume. `MotionJiggle` filters gravity and small accelerometer noise, caps spikes, and resets its gravity baseline on interruption/orientation changes.

The Unity renderer caches rest coordinates and mutable mesh arrays. Deformed normals are rebuilt into reused buffers; idle deformation is skipped. Facial anchors and suspended accents use the same deformation mapping as the body. The authored crown is integrated into its closed body mesh; the comparison baseline retains separate lobes. No network request occurs during touch. `PointerGesture` captures the first pointer and surface, and allows an explicit second toy touch to join a toy gesture. It promotes either surviving finger on release; the controller rebases the remaining drag and eases out the pinch. A plate gesture never upgrades into a pinch. The controller compares the nearest visible-shell hit with the thin plate collider at pointer-down: the toy deforms/lifts, while the turntable parent rotates both toy and plate. Lift translates the whole toy beneath its mount, independently of the plate; hanging gel droops around the grip, and impact velocity drives a bounded squash/jiggle on landing. The existing disk shadow grows and blends toward the ceramic color with height. A nonconvex cylinder mesh collider avoids the oversized capsule produced by nonuniformly scaling a cylinder primitive. UI hits are excluded, store browsing blocks toy input, and lifecycle/orientation interruptions release both fingers.

The custom `.pocklemesh` source records Unity-space geometry and anchors from Blender. `PipCharacterImporter` validates it and creates a native `PipCharacterAsset` plus readable mesh subasset. Resources retains those native assets in builds. `JellyToy.RaycastBody` tests the cached deformed vertices through the portable front-face `SurfaceRaycast` only on pointer-down, rather than recooking a mesh collider during manipulation. The renderer clones the mesh to avoid changing the imported original, welds normals across split UV seams using precomputed logical vertex groups, and attaches the authored facial anchors. The imported `IntegratedCrown` flag suppresses the old separate crown geometry. JSON parsing runs only in the editor importer. FBX and editable `.blend` files are provided for art interchange; runtime loading does not depend on the owner's Blender installation.

`PrototypeHud` and its page partials provide Home, shelf, rewards, box catalog, local profile, Settings, social entry, and play pages in safe-area UGUI layouts. Engine-independent `MenuNavigation` owns Back history and per-page scroll; main tabs reset history without erasing scroll. One daily card is reparented between Rewards and Boxes so claim state stays identical. `GuestProfile` stores local identity separately from collection inventory; `ProfileName` normalizes length/control characters, and labels render user names with rich text disabled. No online identity or social service is created. `ToyPortrait` renders each actual variant once into a 256px RenderTexture and destroys the temporary scene/camera/materials; only the selected toy runs in the viewer. Browsing and dialogs block input and hide the viewer/turntable. `PrototypeStage` reserves the play identity header and gesture hint, keeping at least 3.9 world units vertically and 3 horizontally visible. Comfort settings remain in PlayerPrefs; the new sound volume scales the shared AudioSource while mute keeps its own setting. Profile edits and volume are flushed on navigation, pause/focus loss, and profile edits also on HUD destruction.

`Pockle.Core.CollectionProgress` owns the local beta's four variant counts, 1,000-step daily progress, claim flag, monotonic day, hardware baseline, and pending reveal. `CollectionSession` serializes these fields as one PlayerPrefs JSON value before revealing a reward. `AndroidWalkingTracker` requests activity permission only on the explicit Enable action, then polls the native Android library's hardware step counter. The core handles batches, boot/counter resets, rate rejection, and midnight rebasing. No location or health-data SDK is connected. Production validation and guaranteed background collection are not provided by local saves.

`BoxCatalog` defines provisional consumable IDs, proposed USD prices, and displayed contents/odds. `IBoxCheckout` is the platform integration boundary; its current unavailable adapter cannot charge or award inventory. A real implementation must fetch store prices, validate tokens on the backend, and deduplicate transaction grants before consumption.

`MysteryBox` builds the branded Jelly Garden carton with one shared board mesh, printed exterior/cream interior materials, and lid/dust-flap hinges. Its panel texture is a local Resources asset. `RevealSequence` controls opening, emergence, packaging fade, and the settled handoff; the controller blocks toy input until it finishes. The full reveal takes 2.6 seconds; calm takes 0.85 seconds without rise/scale/bounce. The reveal shader samples the printed artwork, writes solid depth while closed, and disables depth writes during fading. Replay reuses the carton resources, which are released on destruction. Texture import, transparency sorting, and final 3D appearance need local Unity/device checks.

The editor menu configures the prototype's scene and presentation defaults. Build preprocessing guards against a missing scene, render pipeline mismatch, or missing custom shaders. It is not a substitute for an editor build or a real-device check.

The owner’s current collecting direction is daily walking-unlocked boxes or optional paid boxes, with collection-themed packaging. Hardware steps and local daily rewards are implemented for testing; location verification, store billing, and authoritative inventory remain future work. The next viewer experiments are lights-off glow and internal stars/pearls that lag during squeeze/stretch and settle after release. Trading, evolution, and edition registries are outside the simplified initial release.
